I
enter

What happens when the work has no beginning?

The work has already begun before anyone enters and continues after they leave. There is no privileged seat, no master sequence and no single correct view. Arrival becomes an edit point chosen by the visitor.

You enter mid-sentence. The work never tells you where the beginning was.

II
many presents

The same measured hour can contain radically different experiences of duration.

One visitor remains with an image until it changes meaning. Another crosses the same field in seconds. Someone arrives inside an event another person has already left behind. The installation makes those differences visible without forcing them into one timeline.

One space can contain many different nows.

III
the field

Image, sound and architecture become one perceptual environment.

Moving image becomes surface and scale. Spatial sound creates events beyond the field of vision. Darkness hides what comes next. Light determines what appears.

The room is not where the artwork is placed. The room becomes part of the artwork.

spatial research · biological time / linear time

Can an environment change the duration a body feels without changing the duration a clock measures?

The installation develops from research into the difference between linear time and experienced time. Clock time continues at a constant rate, while biological and perceptual time can expand, compress, fragment or seem to suspend according to attention, anticipation, repetition, sensory density and arousal.

Moving image, spatial sound, darkness, light and architecture are treated as instruments for disturbing that relationship. A minute remains a minute, but the experience of that minute is allowed to change.

The experiment is not to represent time. It is to alter how duration is felt.

Sparse movement can make duration expand. Repetition can make the mind anticipate what comes next. Increasing density can compress attention.

V
the science of now

The artwork begins with perception.

The six findings behind Construct of Time are not an explanation added afterwards. They are the factual questions from which the work developed.

01The present is not an instant.

Conscious experience integrates information across a short temporal window.

02Now is assembled.

The brain synchronises sensory signals arriving at different speeds.

03Perception is partly prediction.

The brain anticipates what is likely to happen and corrects those predictions against incoming information.

04Action can begin before awareness.

Neural preparation can precede conscious awareness of a voluntary decision.

05The present can revise the recent past.

Later information can influence how something moments earlier is perceived.

06Clock time is not experienced time.

Attention, anticipation, fear, novelty and memory can stretch or collapse duration.

installation framework · open the spatial notes
01The premise: no fixed beginningopen details ↓

The gallery form removes the stage clock. Visitors arrive at different moments, remain for different lengths of time and assemble their own sequence from the same audiovisual system. The work is continuous; every encounter is partial by design.

02Moving image as materialopen details ↓

Moving image is treated as surface, scale and light rather than as a film that must be watched from beginning to end. Fragments can occupy walls, floor and peripheral vision, allowing the image to behave architecturally.

03Spatial sound and anticipationopen details ↓

Sound can announce an event before it is visible, continue after its source disappears, or make distance physically legible. Spatial sound therefore composes anticipation, memory and orientation rather than simply surrounding the visitor.

04Biological time / clock timeopen details ↓

The installation keeps measured duration constant while changing the conditions under which it is experienced. Density, repetition, darkness, novelty and waiting become variables. The experiment is not to illustrate time but to change how long the same interval feels.

05Site responseopen details ↓

The system responds to architecture rather than concealing it. Corridors produce anticipation. Distance delays arrival. Doorways divide one present from another. Existing surfaces, thresholds and circulation become part of the composition.

06Institutional presentationopen details ↓

The installation is intended for museums, galleries and immersive spaces where darkness, spatial audio and architectural integration can be developed with the venue. Room envelope, projection or screen strategy, sound layout, capacity and circulation remain site-specific technical development.
