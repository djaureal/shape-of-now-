nine-part audiovisual work · three performers · seven screens · cinematic image · architectural light
I
together

What happens when a room gives its attention to the same moment?

Music, moving image, performers, darkness and light unfold as one continuous composition. There is no separation between concert and film. Each element changes the perception of the others, gathering hundreds of individual experiences into a temporary shared present.

For a while, everyone is carried by the same clock.

II
the stage

The film becomes physical architecture.

Seven screens create a fractured cinematic horizon around three performers. Image moves through the structure while light, haze and laser extend its geometry into the room. The performers are not standing in front of the film. They exist inside it.

stage demo · We Measure · 75 sec
III
nine works

Nine chapters approach the same question from different positions.

Measurement becomes rhythm. Memory becomes image. Prediction becomes tension. Repetition changes duration. Each work has its own musical and visual state, but none is intended to stand entirely alone.

Nine works. One movement through time.

IV
moving image

Some ideas are understood differently when they become images.

Bodies dissolve into waves. Architecture loses scale. Measurement becomes geometry. Familiar landscapes become impossible to place. The films do not illustrate the research. They create a world in which the ordinary landmarks of time begin to loosen.

The image does not explain the idea. It allows us to enter it.

V
why live

Understanding that attention changes time is different from feeling it happen.

Sub-bass reaches the body before analysis. Repetition creates expectation. Darkness removes reference. Silence makes seconds noticeable. Then the performance ends, and the moment cannot be repeated exactly because everyone who experienced it has already changed.

Its disappearance is part of the work.
