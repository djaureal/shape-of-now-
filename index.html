CONSTRUCT OF TIMEa work by Tony Funiciello
a lifelong investigation into the experience of time

Construct of Time

live performance · immersive installation
I
elsewhere

We are rarely
where we are.

Attention moves constantly between what happened and what might happen. A memory can pull us decades backwards. An imagined future can change the body before anything has occurred. We call these places past and future, yet every memory, fear, hope and expectation is being experienced in exactly the same place.

Now.

II
the question

So what, exactly,
is now?

The moment we notice it, part of it has already passed. There seems to be no clean boundary where the future becomes the present and the present becomes the past. And yet this almost impossible moment contains everything we have ever experienced.

The closer we look at now, the stranger time becomes.

III
the search

A question became
a lifelong investigation.

Time structures almost everything around us, yet lived experience rarely behaves like a clock. Years disappear. Seconds remain forever. Memory makes something absent feel present. Imagination allows something that has never happened to change a life. The research began as an attempt to understand whether this contradiction was simply philosophical, or whether something deeper was happening inside perception itself.

Science made the question even more remarkable.

IV
the findings · the science of now

What feels immediate is far more constructed than it appears.

The research became the foundation of the artwork. Six findings revealed that perception gathers, predicts, remembers and reorganises information continuously to create the experience we call now.

finding 01

The present is not an instant.

Conscious experience integrates information across a short temporal window.

Even now contains traces of past and future.
finding 02

Now is assembled.

Sensory signals arrive through different pathways and are synchronised into one coherent event.

What feels immediate has already been gathered.
finding 03

Perception is partly prediction.

The brain continuously anticipates what is likely to happen and compares it with incoming information.

Experience is not passive reception.
finding 04

Action can begin before awareness.

Neural preparation can precede conscious awareness of a voluntary decision.

Even the moment of choosing may have a past.
finding 05

The present can revise the recent past.

Later information can alter how an earlier event is perceived.

The past may still be changing inside the present.
finding 06

Clock time is not experienced time.

Attention, anticipation, fear, novelty and memory can stretch or collapse duration.

The clock measures time. The mind experiences something else.
V
authorship

There is only one place
where life can be experienced.

The past survives through memory. The future enters through imagination. Both profoundly shape the present, yet neither can be directly experienced anywhere but now.

We live suspended between memory and possibility, pulled toward what has already happened and what has not happened yet. Yet neither is somewhere else. The past can only return as an experience now. The future can only exist for us as an experience now. We spend our lives reaching backward and forward, without noticing that we never actually leave the present.

Perhaps this is where authorship begins. We cannot control what happened, and we cannot know what will happen next. But we can participate in what it means.

And if meaning can change, then the life being lived can change with it. Perhaps that is where free will has been hiding all along.

VI
the artwork

The research became something that needed to be experienced.

Sound can stretch a moment. Repetition creates expectation. Silence exposes waiting. Moving image can fracture continuity. Light can make something exist for an instant and disappear before the mind has finished understanding it. These are not simply aesthetics. They are instruments for making perception itself perceptible.

The subject is time. The material is our experience of it.
