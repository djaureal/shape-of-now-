I
enter

What happens when the work has no beginning?

The installation is already happening when the visitor arrives and continues after they leave. There is no privileged seat, fixed sequence or single point of view.

You do not watch time unfold. You enter somewhere inside it.

II
many presents

The same work can contain completely different experiences of duration.

One visitor stays with an image for ten minutes. Another crosses it in thirty seconds. Someone enters while somebody else leaves. The physical work remains the same, but its experienced duration changes with every person moving through it.

One space can contain many different nows.

III
the field

Image, sound and architecture become one perceptual environment.

Moving image becomes surface and scale. Spatial sound creates events beyond the field of vision. Darkness hides what comes next. Light determines what appears. Architecture controls what can be discovered at once and what requires movement.

The room is not where the artwork is placed. The room becomes part of the artwork.

IV
duration

Sixty seconds can contain almost nothing, or almost everything.

Sparse movement can make duration expand. Repetition can make the mind anticipate what comes next. Increasing density can compress attention until the visitor stops noticing the clock entirely.

state 01 · duration

Calm / Open

Long intervals and slow visual change allow the present to widen.

state 02 · anticipation

Compression / Pulse

Rhythm and increasing density pull attention toward what comes next.

The measured interval remains unchanged. The person experiencing it does not.

V
the science of now

The artwork begins with perception.

The six findings behind Construct of Time are not an explanation added afterwards. They are the factual questions from which the work developed.

01The present is not an instant.

Conscious experience integrates information across a short temporal window. Even now contains traces of what has just happened and what is arriving.

02By the time now is experienced, it has already happened.

The brain gathers sensory signals arriving at different speeds and synchronises them into one coherent event.

03Perception is partly prediction.

The brain anticipates what is likely to happen and continuously corrects those predictions against incoming information.

04Action can begin before awareness of deciding.

Neural preparation can precede conscious awareness of a voluntary decision. What this means for free will remains debated.

05The present can revise the recent past.

Later information can influence how something moments earlier is perceived. Memory and present experience overlap.

06Clock time is not experienced time.

Attention, anticipation, fear, novelty and memory can stretch or collapse duration.

VI
the film world

The films become material rather than screenings.

Their figures, gestures, geometries and rhythms can be separated from their original cinematic sequence and redistributed through architecture. An image may appear whole in one space and fragmented in another. Sound may reach the visitor before its image.

VII
site response

Every architecture already has its own relationship with time.

A corridor creates anticipation. Distance delays arrival. A doorway divides one experience from another. An empty room slows the body. Construct of Time responds to these existing conditions rather than forcing every venue into one fixed configuration.

The system remains. The experience belongs to the space.
