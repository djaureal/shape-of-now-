one work · another relationship

There is no fixed
point of view.

The live performance gathers a room into one synchronized present. The installation opens that present into space. Visitors move through image, sound and light at their own pace, becoming aware of how attention, memory and anticipation alter duration.

It is not the stage show without performers. It is the same investigation translated into architecture.

the installation language

Image. Sound. Space.
Three forces, one field.

01 · image

Moving-image architecture.

Cinematic and generative image leaves the rectangle of the screen and becomes scale, surface, rhythm and orientation.

02 · sound

Duration becomes physical.

Spatial sound and controlled sub-bass shape attention before the visitor consciously names what has changed.

03 · space / light

Architecture becomes the field.

Light, darkness and the geometry of the room determine how the work is discovered rather than simply where it is displayed.

perceptual states

The same minute
does not always feel the same length.

The installation moves between contrasting states of attention. Calm expands duration. Compression, repetition and anticipation narrow it. These are not decorative moods; they are the material of the work.

state 01 · duration
Calm / Open

Sparse motion, long intervals, low-frequency sound and slow visual change create room for the present to widen.

state 02 · anticipation
Compression / Pulse

Rhythm, repetition and increasing visual density pull attention forward, changing how the same measured duration is experienced.

the science of now

The work begins
with perception.

Six research findings inform the installation's treatment of duration, prediction, memory and attention. The visitor does not need to know the research to experience its consequences.

01The now you feel is not an instant.

Conscious experience appears to integrate a short rolling window rather than a mathematical point. The just-passed and the about-to-be are both folded into what feels present.

02By the time you experience “now”, it is already over.

Signals arrive at the brain at different speeds. Perception aligns them into a coherent event, meaning the experienced present is already a construction.

03Perception is partly prediction.

The brain continually anticipates incoming information and revises those predictions against sensory signals. The world we experience is actively assembled.

04Action can begin before conscious awareness of deciding.

Readiness-potential experiments opened an enduring debate about the relationship between neural preparation, conscious intention and free will.

05The brain can edit the recent past.

Postdictive visual effects show that later information can influence what we report having perceived moments earlier. Memory and present experience overlap.

06Arousal changes remembered duration.

Time is not read from a single internal clock. Attention, bodily state and memory density all contribute to how long an interval feels.

adaptation to architecture

The system remains.
The room changes it.

Construct of Time is conceived as site-responsive rather than locked to one room. Image placement, sound configuration, movement path, lighting and scale can respond to the architecture while the conceptual system and visual language remain intact.

This allows the installation to inhabit museums, galleries and immersive spaces without becoming a generic touring package.
